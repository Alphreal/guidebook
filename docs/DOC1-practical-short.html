Online Learning Effectiveness Guide for Our Class

Asia University — First-Year Students (Practical Short Version)

How to use this guide (2 minutes):

2. Go straight to that chapter. Do the 5-minute routine first.

Chapter 1 — Self-Regulation & Time

Tonight (5 min): SMART tonight. Write ONE study goal for tomorrow in this shape:

Specific — Measurable — Achievable — Relevant — Time-bound.

Example: "Finish LMS Unit 3 quiz (20 questions) before 9pm tomorrow, score 70%+."

Tomorrow morning (5 min): ALPEN plan. List tasks, estimate minutes, add buffer, decide order, check off at night.

Study block: 25-5 x2. 25 minutes focused work, 5 minutes break, repeat once. Then a longer 15-minute break.

Why it works (3 lines): Self-regulated learners plan, monitor, and adjust in a cycle (Zimmerman). Specific goals beat vague intentions (SMART). Short spaced sessions with recall beat re-reading (Roediger 2006; Dunlosky 2013).

Chapter 2 — Distraction & Concentration

Routine (5 min setup, use all week): Phone in a drawer (not face-down on desk), ONE browser tab open, all notifications OFF during study blocks.

Why it works (3 lines): A phone nearby drains thinking power even when untouched (Brain Drain, Ward 2017). Heavy multitaskers perform worse at switching (Ophir, Nass & Wagner 2009). Every glance back at messages leaves "attention residue" that slows you down.

Chapter 3 — Interaction & Support

Routine: Use this template every time you are stuck for more than 15 minutes:

"I tried ___ . I expected ___ but got ___ . My question is ___ ."

Then: (1) peer-check with one classmate, (2) post it in the class channel / forum.

Why it works (3 lines): Learning works best when teaching, social, and thinking presence meet (Community of Inquiry, Garrison). Asking early shrinks the distance in online study (Moore). Clear questions get faster, better help (Martin & Bolliger 2018).

Chapter 4 — Fatigue & Technology

Routine: Study 50 minutes, move 10. Every 20 minutes, look 20 meters away for 20 seconds (20-20-20). Before each online meeting: test internet, mic, camera (2-minute pre-check).

Why it works (3 lines): Back-to-back video calls overload the brain (Zoom fatigue, Bailenson). Tech stress builds when tools feel out of control (Saleem 2024). Light, sleep, and breaks protect focus (Huberman); short segments beat marathons (segmenting principle).
